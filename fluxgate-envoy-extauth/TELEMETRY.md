Published-policy Envoy requests retain authoritative Mongo policy reads and the original Redis decision. Only exact built-in Mongo rate-limit event recording is adapted; durable policy publication and its audit remain synchronous.

Telemetry has two bounded queues. A fixed four-worker dispatcher admits at most 1024 queued captures without blocking callers. It preserves synchronous execution of other recorders and custom subclasses; only exact built-in Composite and Mongo recorder types are adapted. Events freeze JSON-compatible nested request attributes; unsupported, cyclic, or oversized attributes are rejected. Capture is limited to 512 nodes, depth 32, and 64 KiB of scalar UTF-8 data including context fields, headers, keys and numbers; the original decision is unaffected. Dispatcher workers forward captures to the LTS Mongo recorder, whose own single daemon writer admits at most 10,000 queued documents by default and uses unordered `insertMany` batches of up to 500. A normal return from `record()` can represent a queued, dropped, or failed-to-build document; it does not mean Mongo persisted it. Event timestamps represent document construction time, and concurrent forwarding does not preserve request order. Queue overflow, writer errors, process loss, or shutdown can lose events; this is best-effort telemetry, not a durable audit guarantee.

The existing private management listener exposes GET /telemetry with anonymous numeric counters. `accepted`, `forwarded`, `failed`, `rejected`, `cancelled`, `inflight`, `queue`, and `outstanding` describe the dispatcher: `forwarded` counts normal leaf `record()` returns, `failed` counts thrown leaf calls, and `outstanding` is `accepted - forwarded - failed - cancelled`. The previous ambiguous `succeeded` field is replaced by `forwarded`. An empty dispatcher queue or zero dispatcher outstanding does not imply persistence.

`writerWritten`, `writerDropped`, `writerFailed`, and `writerPending` aggregate the underlying LTS Mongo recorder counters. Each adapted built-in recorder is counted once by identity, even when it occurs in multiple composites. These are recorder-lifetime totals and include direct calls made outside this dispatcher. `writerWritten` reflects documents counted as written by the recorder; `writerPending` includes queued and active writer work. They are independent atomic reads, so a response during concurrent activity is an approximate snapshot rather than an accounting transaction. `writerFailed` covers document-construction and Mongo write failures; `writerDropped` covers recorder overflow or closure. No identities, keys, documents, or exception messages are exposed by this endpoint. When the published-policy dispatcher is absent, the response is {"enabled":0}. GET /diagnostics is unchanged.

The dispatcher uses lifecycle phase 0, after Spring Boot web draining and before bean destruction. It depends on `FluxgateMongoClientHolder`, preserving isolation from the application's `MongoClient` beans. Shutdown waits at most ten seconds for dispatch, then cancels queued captures and interrupts active tasks. Tasks that ignore interruption remain outstanding until they finish. During subsequent bean destruction, the built-in recorder closes before its collection/database/holder dependencies, attempting to flush its own queue for five seconds and allowing up to one additional second after interruption. The holder closes an owned FluxGate client afterward. These bounded waits provide no guarantee that an uninterruptible store call ends before client closure. Deployment termination time must budget web/pre-stop draining, the ten-second dispatcher drain, the recorder's five-second flush plus interruption grace, and process margin.
