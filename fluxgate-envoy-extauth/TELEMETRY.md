Published-policy Envoy requests retain authoritative Mongo policy reads and the original Redis decision. Only exact built-in Mongo rate-limit event recording runs asynchronously; durable policy publication and its audit remain synchronous and unchanged.

A fixed four-worker dispatcher admits at most 1024 queued events without blocking callers. It preserves synchronous execution of other recorders and custom subclasses; only exact built-in Composite and Mongo recorder types are adapted. Events freeze JSON-compatible nested request attributes; unsupported, cyclic, or oversized attributes are rejected. Capture is limited to 512 nodes, depth 32, and 64 KiB of scalar UTF-8 data including context fields, headers, keys and numbers; the original decision is unaffected. Event timestamps represent writer processing time, and concurrent delivery does not preserve order. Queue overflow, writer errors, process loss, or shutdown can lose events; this is best-effort telemetry, not a durable audit guarantee.

The existing private management listener exposes GET /telemetry with anonymous numeric accepted, succeeded, failed, rejected, cancelled, inflight, queue, and outstanding counters. No identities, keys, documents, or exception messages are exposed. Outstanding includes unfinished active work; an empty queue alone does not mean delivery completed. When the published-policy dispatcher is absent, the response is {"enabled":0}. GET /diagnostics is unchanged.

The writer uses lifecycle phase 0, after Spring Boot web draining and before its dependent Mongo client is destroyed. Shutdown waits at most ten seconds, then explicitly cancels queued tasks and interrupts active tasks. Tasks that ignore interruption remain counted as outstanding until they finish; shutdown does not claim successful delivery. Deployment termination time must budget web/pre-stop draining plus this ten-second writer drain and process margin.
